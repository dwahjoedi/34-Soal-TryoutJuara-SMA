Dalam informasi PSB Labschool, Bahasa Inggris tidak selalu disebut sebagai instrumen terpisah seperti Kuantitatif atau Pemahaman Baca. ## 2. Reading Comprehension: Evidence First

Reading comprehension is not translating every word. It is understanding what the text says, what it implies, and why the writer wrote it. If you try to translate word by word, you may get tired before finding the answer.

Start with the question. If it asks for a detail, find the sentence that says it. If it asks for inference, collect clues. If it asks for main idea, ask what the whole text is mostly about.

Example:

> Please bring your umbrella. The sky has been dark since morning.

The text does not directly say "It will rain." But the clues support the inference that rain is possible. That is not random guessing. That is evidence-based inference.

**Gambar 1 - Reading Evidence Map**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat ilustrasi reading passage pendek dengan kalimat "Please bring your umbrella. The sky has been dark since morning." Sorot "umbrella" dan "dark sky" sebagai clues. Di kanan tampilkan kotak "Inference: It may rain." Tambahkan panah dari clues ke inference. Sertakan peringatan kecil: "Inference must come from evidence." Gaya clean, seperti worksheet reading SMP.

### Common Question Types

- **Main idea:** asks what the whole text is mostly about. Avoid answers that only mention one detail.
- **Detail:** asks specific information. Go back to the text and find proof.
- **Inference:** asks what is probably true. Use clues, not personal imagination.
- **Purpose:** asks why the writer wrote the text: to inform, invite, warn, describe, tell, or persuade.
- **Reference:** asks what a pronoun refers to. Read the previous sentence carefully.

### Worked Example 1 - Main Idea

> "Many students prefer cycling to school. It saves money, keeps the body active, and reduces traffic near the school gate."

The main idea is not only "cycling saves money." That is one detail. The better main idea is: cycling to school gives several benefits.

### Worked Example 2 - Reference

> "Mira borrowed Sinta's dictionary because she left hers at home."

The word "hers" refers to Sinta's dictionary? No. Read the sentence carefully. Mira borrowed the dictionary because Mira left her own dictionary at home. So "hers" refers to Mira's dictionary.

### How to Read Without Translating Every Word

If you try to translate every word, you may lose time and forget the question. Use this order:

1. Read the question first.
2. Identify what it asks: main idea, detail, inference, purpose, or reference.
3. Scan the passage for keywords.
4. Read the sentence around the keyword carefully.
5. Choose the answer that has evidence.

For main idea questions, do not stop at the first sentence if the text develops an idea. Read the whole paragraph quickly and ask: "What do all sentences support?"

For inference questions, avoid answers that are emotionally too strong. If a text says "Rina looked down and spoke softly," we may infer she is nervous or sad. But we cannot infer she hates everyone unless the text supports it.

### Common Wrong Answers in Reading

- **Too broad:** the answer talks about a bigger topic than the passage.
- **Too narrow:** the answer only mentions one detail.
- **Not stated:** the answer may be true in real life but not supported by the text.
- **Opposite meaning:** the option changes a key word such as can/cannot, always/sometimes, before/after.
- **Keyword trap:** the option repeats words from the text but changes the meaning.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- find evidence in the text;
- distinguish main idea from detail;
- explain an inference using clues;
- identify what pronouns refer to.

## 3. Vocabulary in Context

One English word can have more than one meaning. The word "light" can mean not heavy, not dark, or something that helps us see. The sentence decides the meaning.

When a question asks the closest meaning of a word, do not answer from memory only. Read one sentence before and after the word. Then replace the word with each option. The best option should keep the sentence natural and logical.

**Gambar 1 - Context Clues Around a Word**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat ilustrasi kalimat bahasa Inggris dengan kata "light" di tengah. Buat tiga cabang makna: "not heavy", "not dark", dan "lamp/brightness". Tampilkan tiga contoh kalimat kecil: "This bag is light", "The room is light", "Turn on the light". Sorot konteks yang membuat makna berbeda. Gaya clean, edukatif, cocok untuk siswa SMP.

### Common Clues

| Clue Type | How It Helps | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Example clue | gives examples after the word | fruits such as apples and bananas |
| Contrast clue | uses but, however, although | small but strong |
| Cause-effect clue | shows reason/result | because, so, therefore |
| Description clue | explains the word nearby | a huge animal with a long trunk |
| Tone clue | positive/negative feeling | wonderful, terrible, dangerous |

### Worked Example - Word Meaning

> "The test was quite challenging, but Riko did not give up."
>
> What is the closest meaning of "challenging"?

The word "but" shows contrast: the test was difficult, but Riko kept trying. So "challenging" means difficult, not fun or short.

### Worked Example - Part of Speech

If the word in the question is an adjective, the answer is usually also an adjective. If the sentence says "She gave a brief explanation," then "brief" describes explanation. The closest meaning is "short", not "quickly" because "quickly" is an adverb.

### Word Formation and Collocation

Word formation means how a word changes form. For example, **happy** becomes **happiness**, **care** becomes **careful** or **careless**, and **teach** becomes **teacher**. In PSB-style questions, word formation usually appears in a sentence with a blank.

Collocation means words that naturally go together. English often says **make a decision**, **do homework**, **take a photo**, and **heavy rain**. If you translate word by word from Indonesian, the phrase may sound unnatural.

**Gambar 2 - Word Formation and Collocation**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat ilustrasi dua panel untuk siswa SMP. Panel kiri "Word formation" menampilkan kata dasar "care" bercabang menjadi "careful", "careless", dan "carefully" dengan label adjective/adverb. Panel kanan "Collocation" menampilkan pasangan kata natural: make a decision, do homework, take a photo, heavy rain. Beri tanda silang pada pasangan tidak natural seperti "do a decision". Gaya clean, edukatif, cocok untuk kelas 7-9.

### Worked Example - Word Formation

> You must be ... when crossing the road.

The blank needs an adjective after "be". From the word "care", the best form is **careful**. "Carefully" is an adverb, so it does not fit after "be" in this sentence.

### Worked Example - Collocation

> I need to ... my homework before dinner.

The natural phrase is **do my homework**, not *make my homework*. This is collocation: the grammar may look simple, but the word pair must sound natural.

### How to Guess Meaning Safely

When you meet an unfamiliar word, do not panic. You can often guess enough meaning from clues.

Example:

> The room was **spacious**. There was enough space for thirty students to sit comfortably.

The second sentence explains the first. "Enough space" tells us that spacious means large or roomy.

Example with contrast:

> The task looked simple, but it was actually **complicated**.

"But" shows contrast. If it looked simple but was actually complicated, then complicated means not simple or difficult.

### Word Family Patterns

Many English words form a family:

| Verb | Noun | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| decide | decision | decisive | decisively |
| educate | education | educational | educationally |
| protect | protection | protective | protectively |
| care | care | careful/careless | carefully/carelessly |

You do not need to memorize every form. Train yourself to ask what the blank needs. After "be", you often need an adjective. After an adjective, you often need a noun. After a verb, you may need an adverb.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- read words around the target word;
- identify positive, negative, or neutral meaning;
- match part of speech;
- avoid literal translation when context changes the meaning;
- recognize simple word formation;
- choose common collocations.

## 4. Grammar in Context

Grammar is not a collection of formulas to memorize. Grammar tells time, relationship, certainty, quantity, and meaning. In PSB-style questions, the correct grammar usually depends on the situation.

Time signals are useful:

| Signal | Common Tense/Structure |
|---|---|
| every day, usually, always | simple present |
| now, at the moment | present continuous |
| yesterday, last week | simple past |
| tomorrow, next month | future |
| since, for | present perfect |
| while, when + past event | past continuous + simple past |
| at this time tomorrow | future continuous |
| if + simple present | will/can/may + verb 1 |

> **Present perfect vs simple past — the key difference:**
> - **Simple past** = the action is finished and in the past, with no direct connection to now. "She **went** to Bali last year." (It's over.)
> - **Present perfect** = the action happened in the past, but its result or relevance still connects to the present moment. "She **has lived** here since 2020." (She still lives here now.) "I **have lost** my pen." (I still don't have it now.)
>
> Signal words "since" (from a point in time) and "for" (duration) almost always signal present perfect, not simple past. "I lived here for five years" means it is over; "I have lived here for five years" means I am still here.

**Gambar 1 - Time Signal to Tense Timeline**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat timeline grammar sederhana untuk siswa SMP. Tampilkan tiga area: past, present, future. Letakkan contoh time signals: "yesterday" di past, "now" di present happening now, "every day" sebagai habit, "tomorrow" di future, dan "since 2020" dari past menuju now. Tambahkan contoh kalimat pendek untuk setiap sinyal. Gaya clean, warna berbeda untuk tiap waktu.

### Common Grammar Traps

- **Modals:** can, must, should, may selalu diikuti verb 1: "can go", bukan "can goes".
- **Subject-verb agreement:** "He walks", tetapi "They walk".
- **Article:** "a" untuk bunyi konsonan, "an" untuk bunyi vokal.
- **Preposition:** "interested in", "good at", "afraid of" sering muncul sebagai pasangan tetap.
- **Pronoun:** pronoun harus cocok dengan orang/benda yang dirujuk.

### Worked Example 1 - Time Signal

> Rina usually ... breakfast at 6 a.m.

"Usually" menunjukkan kebiasaan. Subjek "Rina" adalah she, jadi verb simple present memakai -s.

Jawaban: **has**.

### Worked Example 2 - Modal

> You must ... your assignment today.

Setelah modal "must", gunakan verb 1. Jawaban: **submit**, bukan submits atau submitted.

### Worked Example 3 - Past Continuous

> I ... dinner when my friend called.

The sentence has one longer action and one interrupting action. The longer action is **was having dinner**, and the interrupting action is **called**.

### Worked Example 4 - Future Continuous

> At this time tomorrow, we ... our English test.

"At this time tomorrow" describes an action in progress in the future, so the answer is **will be taking**. For SMP-level PSB preparation, the key is recognizing the time signal, not memorizing a long list of tense names.

### Grammar Decision Tree

When grammar options look confusing, use this order:

1. Find the subject.
2. Find the time signal.
3. Decide whether the action is habit, happening now, finished past, connected to now, or future.
4. Check whether there is a modal.
5. Check whether the sentence needs active or passive meaning.

Example:

> The classroom ... every Friday.

If the options are "cleans", "cleaned", "is cleaned", and "cleaning", ask who does the action. The classroom does not clean itself. The meaning is passive: someone cleans the classroom. With "every Friday", the answer is **is cleaned**.

### Grammar Traps That Often Appear

- After **to**, use verb 1: "to study", not "to studying".
- After **enjoy**, use verb-ing: "enjoy reading".
- After **must/can/should**, use verb 1.
- With **there is/there are**, match singular/plural noun.
- With **because**, give a reason. With **so**, give a result.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- use time signals to choose tense;
- use verb 1 after modals;
- check subject and verb;
- choose prepositions based on common expression;
- recognize past continuous from interrupted past actions;
- recognize future continuous from a future time-in-progress signal.

## 5. Dialogue and Interpersonal Expressions

Dialogue questions test communication. A sentence can be grammatically correct but still wrong because it does not fit the situation. If someone apologizes, you should respond politely. If someone invites you, you accept or decline naturally.

**Gambar 1 - Dialogue Intention Map**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat diagram percakapan bahasa Inggris. Di tengah tulis "What is the speaker doing?" Cabang-cabangnya: thanking, apologizing, inviting, suggesting, asking opinion, offering help. Untuk tiap cabang, beri contoh respons pendek: "You're welcome", "That's okay", "I'd love to", "You should...", "I think...", "Can I help you?" Gaya clean, cocok untuk siswa kelas 7-9.

### Common Patterns

| Situation | Natural Response |
|---|---|
| Thank you | You're welcome / No problem |
| Sorry | That's okay / Never mind |
| Invitation | I'd love to / Sorry, I can't |
| Suggestion | That's a good idea / I don't think so |
| Asking opinion | I think... / In my opinion... |
| Offering help | Yes, please / No, thanks |
| Asking direction | Go straight / Turn left / It is next to... |
| Permission | May I...? / Sure / Sorry, you can't |
| Congratulating | Congratulations! / Thank you |
| Hope/wish | I hope... / I wish... |

### Worked Example - Invitation

> A: Would you like to join our study group this afternoon?  
> B: ...

A natural accepting response is "I'd love to." A response like "You're welcome" is polite, but it answers thanks, not invitation.

### Worked Example - Direction and Permission

> A: Excuse me, how can I get to the library?  
> B: ...

The response should give direction, such as "Go straight and turn left." A response like "Congratulations" is grammatically fine as a sentence, but it does not answer the question.

For permission:

> A: May I borrow your dictionary?  
> B: ...

A natural response is "Sure, here you are" or "Sorry, I still need it." The key is matching the function: asking permission needs allowing or refusing politely.

**Gambar 2 - Matching Dialogue Function to Response**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat infografik pasangan fungsi dialog bahasa Inggris. Tampilkan kolom kiri "Speaker's intention" berisi asking direction, asking permission, congratulating, expressing hope. Kolom kanan berisi contoh respons natural: "Go straight and turn left", "Sure, here you are", "Congratulations!", "I hope you win." Hubungkan tiap pasangan dengan garis. Tambahkan satu contoh respons salah yang tidak cocok konteks. Gaya clean, cocok untuk siswa SMP.

### Tone and Politeness

Dialogue questions often test politeness. A direct sentence can be correct grammar but too rude for the context.

Compare:

> Give me your pen.

and:

> Could I borrow your pen, please?

Both communicate a request, but the second is more polite. If the situation is with a teacher, guest, or older person, choose the more polite response. If it is between close friends, the response can be more casual, but still must fit the intention.

### Common Dialogue Traps

- Answering thanks with an apology response.
- Answering invitation with "You're welcome".
- Giving opinion when the speaker asks for direction.
- Choosing a response that is too harsh for a polite situation.
- Ignoring whether the speaker accepts or refuses.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- identify the speaker's intention;
- choose a socially natural response;
- reject answers that are grammatically correct but contextually wrong.

## 6. Functional Texts

Functional texts are short texts made for action. A notice informs, a warning warns, an invitation invites, a label gives product information, and a procedure explains steps.

The key is to ask: **What should the reader know or do after reading this text?**

**Gambar 1 - Functional Text Purpose Board**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat infografik papan tujuan functional text. Tampilkan lima kartu: Notice = informs rule/info, Warning = warns danger, Invitation = invites someone, Label = gives product details, Procedure = tells steps. Tambahkan ikon sederhana untuk masing-masing. Beri contoh kecil "Keep off the grass" di kartu warning/notice dengan catatan "reader should not step on the grass." Gaya clean dan rapi.

### Common Question Types

- What is the purpose of the text?
- Who is the text for?
- What should the reader do?
- Where can you find this text?
- Which statement is true based on the text?
- What does a word on the label mean?

### Worked Example - Notice

> NOTICE: Library books must be returned before Friday.

The purpose is to inform a rule. The key word "must" shows obligation. If an option says "Students may return books anytime", it is wrong because "before Friday" limits the time.

### Reading Short Texts Like Instructions

Functional text is short, so every word matters. Words like **must**, **only**, **free**, **before**, **after**, **do not**, and **for members** can change the answer.

Example:

> Swimming pool is open for members only from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Important details:

- who: members only;
- what: swimming pool access;
- when: 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.;
- limit: not for non-members.

If an option says "Everyone can use the pool at night," it is wrong because both "members only" and the time limit are violated.

### Text Type Clues

- A **notice** usually gives information or rules.
- A **warning** tells danger.
- A **label** gives product details such as ingredients, expiry date, or directions.
- An **invitation** gives event, time, place, and invited person.
- A **procedure** uses ordered steps and action verbs.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- identify the purpose of a short text;
- notice words like must, only, before, after, do not;
- find the target reader;
- separate important details from decoration.

## 7. Narrative and Descriptive Text

Narrative text tells a story. It usually has characters, setting, problem, events, and resolution. Descriptive text describes a person, place, animal, or thing using details.

In narrative questions, focus on why characters act, how the problem changes, and what lesson can be learned. In descriptive questions, focus on specific features and sensory details.

**Gambar 1 - Narrative Story Arc**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat diagram alur cerita sederhana untuk narrative text. Tampilkan lima titik: orientation, problem, events, climax, resolution. Beri contoh kecil: "A student loses a book -> searches everywhere -> asks friends -> finds it in the library -> learns to be more careful." Gaya clean, seperti peta cerita untuk siswa SMP.

### Worked Example - Character Trait

> "Although the rain was heavy, Dito stayed after school to help the janitor move the books."

The best trait is helpful or responsible. Do not choose "lazy" because the action shows the opposite.

### Worked Example - Descriptive Detail

If a text says "The old library has tall wooden shelves, wide windows, and a quiet reading corner," the text describes the library's appearance and atmosphere. The main focus is not a story event, but details about a place.

### Narrative: Track the Change

A narrative usually starts with a normal situation, then a problem appears, then characters respond, and finally something changes. To understand the story, ask:

1. What was the problem?
2. What did the character do?
3. What changed at the end?
4. What lesson can be learned?

If the question asks for moral value, do not choose a lesson that sounds nice but does not match the story. If the story is about a student returning a lost wallet, the moral may be honesty, not bravery.

### Descriptive: Build a Mental Picture

Descriptive text gives details so the reader can imagine something. Details may describe color, size, shape, sound, smell, location, or atmosphere.

If the question asks "What is the place like?", look for adjectives and descriptive phrases. If the question asks "Where is the object?", look for prepositions such as beside, behind, between, near, or in front of.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- identify story problem and resolution;
- infer character traits from actions;
- distinguish narrative events from descriptive details;
- choose the best title based on the whole text.

## 9. Final Exam Mindset

Do not panic when you see a long English text. You do not need to understand every single word. First, understand the question. Then find the part of the text that gives evidence.

Fast strategy:

- For reading, go back to the passage.
- For vocabulary, use context clues.
- For grammar, read the time signal and sentence meaning.
- For dialogue, identify the speaker's intention.
- For functional text, ask what the reader should do.
- For narrative, follow the problem and resolution.

The safest answer is the one that fits the text, the grammar, and the situation at the same time.

## 10. Passive Voice and Conditional Sentences

These two grammar points often appear in PSB-style questions and are sometimes not fully practiced at the SMP level. Understanding them through meaning — not memorization — is the key.

### Passive Voice: When the Action Matters More Than the Doer

In most sentences, we say who does something (active). In passive voice, we say what happens to something (passive). The doer can be mentioned with "by" or left out entirely.

Active: **Students clean the classroom every Friday.**  
Passive: **The classroom is cleaned every Friday.**

Both sentences talk about the same event. The focus shifts from "students" to "the classroom."

**Gambar 1 - Active vs Passive: Who Is the Focus?**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat ilustrasi dua panel untuk siswa SMP. Panel kiri "Active Voice": gambar siswa aktif membersihkan kelas, subjek "Students" disorot dengan kotak. Label kalimat: "Students clean the classroom." Panel kanan "Passive Voice": gambar kelas yang sudah bersih, subjek "The classroom" disorot. Label kalimat: "The classroom is cleaned." Tambahkan catatan: "be + V3 = passive." Gaya clean, edukatif, background putih.

### How to Build Passive Voice

> **What is V3?** In English, most verbs have three forms:
> - **V1** = base form (present): *clean, write, send*
> - **V2** = simple past: *cleaned, wrote, sent*
> - **V3** = past participle: *cleaned, written, sent*
>
> For **regular verbs**, V2 and V3 are the same: *clean → cleaned → cleaned*.
> For **irregular verbs**, V3 is often different from V2: *write → wrote → written*; *go → went → gone*; *break → broke → broken*.
>
> In passive voice, the formula is always **be + V3** (past participle). The "be" changes based on tense (is/was/will be/has been), but V3 stays the same.

| Tense | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | is/are + V3 | The room **is cleaned** every day. |
| Simple Past | was/were + V3 | The window **was broken** yesterday. |
| Present Perfect | has/have been + V3 | The letter **has been sent**. |
| Simple Future | will be + V3 | The results **will be announced** tomorrow. |

> **Exam tip:** When you see "by + someone" in a sentence, look for the passive structure before it. If the subject cannot logically do the action, the verb should be passive.

### Worked Example 1 — Choosing Active or Passive

> The new school library ... last month.

A. built  
B. was built  
C. has built  
D. builds

The subject is "the new school library." Libraries do not build themselves — someone built the library. The meaning is passive. "Last month" signals simple past. So the answer is **was built**.  
**Answer: B**

### Worked Example 2 — Passive with "By"

> The winning poem ... by Dian at the school festival.

A. wrote  
B. is written  
C. was written  
D. has written

"By Dian" signals passive. The event happened at the school festival (past). So **was written** fits.  
**Answer: C**

### Conditional Sentences: If This Happens, Then That Follows

Conditional sentences talk about situations and their results. For SMP PSB preparation, two types are most useful.

**Type 1 — Real and Possible (Present → Future)**

Pattern: **If + simple present, will/can/may + verb 1**

> If you study hard, **you will pass** the exam.  
> If it rains tomorrow, **we will cancel** the trip.

This describes something that can really happen.

**Type 2 — Unreal or Hypothetical (Imagining differently)**

Pattern: **If + simple past, would/could + verb 1**

> If I **were** a teacher, I **would make** lessons more fun.  
> If she **had** more time, she **could finish** the project.

Note: "were" is used for all subjects in Type 2 (not "was"), especially in formal and test contexts.

**Gambar 2 - Type 1 vs Type 2 Conditional**

**Prompt ilustrasi:**  
Buat ilustrasi dua jalur kondisional untuk siswa SMP. Jalur kiri berjudul "Type 1 – Real Possibility": seseorang belajar keras → lulus ujian. Label kalimat: "If you study, you will pass." Jalur kanan berjudul "Type 2 – Hypothetical": seseorang membayangkan skenario berbeda, seperti menjadi lebih kaya. Label: "If I had more money, I would travel." Gunakan warna berbeda untuk setiap tipe dan tambahkan label pada kata kunci (will vs would). Gaya clean, background putih.

### Common Signals

| Signal Word | What It Suggests |
|---|---|
| If + present tense → | Type 1: use "will" |
| If + past tense (were/had) → | Type 2: use "would" |
| "I wish..." + past tense | Imagining something different now |
| "I hope..." + present/future | Real and possible |

> **Why does "I wish" use past tense?** English uses past tense in "wish" sentences to signal that the situation is *unreal or opposite to the current reality* — not that it happened in the past.
> - "I **wish** I **had** more time." → I don't have enough time right now. (Past tense = signal of unreality, not past time.)
> - "I **hope** I **will have** more time." → It's still possible. (Present/future = real possibility.)
>
> Think of it this way: **wish = you're imagining a different reality; hope = you're expecting a real possibility.**

### Worked Example 3 — Type 1

> If she ... harder, she will do better on the test.

A. study  
B. studies  
C. studied  
D. will study

After "if" in Type 1, use simple present. Subject "she" → third person → **studies**.  
**Answer: B**

### Worked Example 4 — Type 2

> If I ... more time, I would finish this project today.

A. have  
B. had  
C. has  
D. will have

"Would finish" signals Type 2 → "if + simple past." Subject "I" → **had**.  
**Answer: B**

### Common Grammar Traps: Passive and Conditionals Together

Sometimes questions combine both:

> If the announcement **had been made** earlier, many students **would have attended**.

This is an advanced form (Type 3 conditional + passive), but for SMP PSB, knowing the core patterns above is enough.

### Mini-Check

Make sure you can:

- identify passive voice by looking for "be + V3";
- choose the correct "be" form (is/was/will be/has been) based on tense;
- distinguish Type 1 from Type 2 by looking at the verb form after "if";
- complete conditional sentences without just guessing — look for the time signal.

**Question 1 — Main Idea**

> "The school garden project has become more than just a gardening activity. Students learn about ecology, teamwork, and responsibility. Teachers use the garden to make science lessons more meaningful. Parents have even joined weekend sessions to help."
>
> What is the main idea of the passage?

A. The school garden is a good place to grow vegetables.  
B. The school garden project benefits many groups and builds multiple skills.  
C. Science lessons are taught in the school garden.  
D. Parents help teachers with the school garden project.

**Discussion:** A mentions vegetables — not stated. C and D are only single details. B covers the whole passage: multiple benefits for multiple groups.  
**Answer: B**

---

**Question 2 — Passive Voice**

> The announcement ... to all students before the assembly ended.

A. was made  
B. has made  
C. made  
D. make

**Discussion:** "The announcement" does not make itself. Passive + simple past → **was made**.  
**Answer: A**

---

**Question 3 — Vocabulary in Context**

> "The lecture was so **tedious** that many students began to lose focus after only ten minutes."
>
> The word "tedious" most closely means...

A. exciting  
B. long  
C. boring  
D. confusing

**Discussion:** Students lost focus = negative reaction. "So tedious that" shows it caused a problem. The word must be something negative → **boring** fits best.  
**Answer: C**

---

**Question 4 — Dialogue: Responding to Apology**

> A: I'm really sorry for breaking your ruler.  
> B: ...

A. It's okay. Don't worry about it.  
B. Thank you so much!  
C. I'm fine, thanks.  
D. You're welcome.

**Discussion:** A apologizes → B should accept the apology politely. "It's okay" is the right response. "You're welcome" responds to thanks, not apologies.  
**Answer: A**

---

**Question 5 — Conditional Type 2**

> "If I ... enough money, I would travel around Southeast Asia."

A. have  
B. had  
C. has  
D. having

**Discussion:** "Would travel" signals Type 2 → "if + simple past." Subject "I" → **had**.  
**Answer: B**

**Question 6 — Functional Text: Notice**

> NOTICE: The swimming pool is closed for maintenance from Monday to Wednesday. Pool users are advised to use the gym facilities during this period.
>
> What can we conclude from the notice?

A. The gym will also be closed next week.  
B. Pool users may use the gym instead during the pool closure.  
C. The pool will be closed permanently.  
D. Only gym members can use the swimming pool.

**Discussion:** The notice says to use the gym during maintenance. B is the only statement directly supported by the text. A, C, D add information not in the notice.  
**Answer: B**

**Question 7 — Word Formation**

> She showed great ... in handling the difficult situation calmly.

A. patient  
B. patience  
C. patiently  
D. patients

**Discussion:** After "great," a noun is needed. "Patience" is the noun form. "Patient" is an adjective or noun for a sick person. "Patiently" is an adverb.  
**Answer: B**

**Question 8 — Inference**

> "Riko looked at the clock three times and kept tapping his fingers on the desk. His friend leaned over and whispered, 'Relax, there's still five minutes.'"
>
> What can be inferred about Riko?

A. Riko is bored and wants to leave.  
B. Riko feels nervous or impatient about something.  
C. Riko is angry at his friend.  
D. Riko is checking the clock because he is tired.

**Discussion:** Checking the clock + tapping fingers = signs of anxiety or impatience. The friend's response "Relax" also confirms Riko seems tense. B is the most supported inference.  
**Answer: B**

**Question 9 — Passive Voice + Tense**

> The school's science laboratory ... completely last year to include new equipment.

A. renovated  
B. renovates  
C. was renovated  
D. is renovating

**Discussion:** "Laboratory" (subject) cannot renovate itself → passive. "Last year" = simple past → **was renovated**.  
**Answer: C**
