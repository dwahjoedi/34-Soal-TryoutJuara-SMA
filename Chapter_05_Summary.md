# Chapter 5 — Leadership Skills
### Key Concepts Summary (Rita Mulcahy, 11th Edition)

---

## 1. Overview of Leadership

Project managers must both **manage** and **lead** — with emphasis on leadership to help the team and stakeholders perform at their best. There is no single right way to lead; the approach must be tailored to each situation.

### Management vs. Leadership

| Management Focus | Leadership Focus |
|---|---|
| Tasks / things | People |
| Control | Empowerment |
| Efficiency | Effectiveness |
| Doing things right | Doing the right things |
| Speed | Direction |
| Practices | Principles |
| Command | Communication |

> **Exam tip:** Leadership focuses on people; management focuses on tasks. A PM must do both.

---

## 2. Critical Thinking

The PM should apply critical thinking to effectively manage a project — looking at it from all angles and choosing the best path:
- Gathering unbiased information
- Responding logically, without added emotion
- Resolving issues using analytical skills
- Being aware of relationships and patterns
- Identifying flawed reasoning

---

## 3. Emotional Intelligence (EI)

The ability to **perceive, evaluate, and control emotions** in self and others. EI can be learned and developed.

### Four Quadrants of Emotional Intelligence

| | **What I understand** | **What I do** |
|---|---|---|
| **With me** | **Self-awareness**: self-confidence, accurate self-assessment, emotional self-awareness | **Self-management**: self-control, conscientiousness, adaptability |
| **With others** | **Social awareness**: empathy, organizational awareness, environmental awareness | **Relationship management**: influence, develop, inspire, teamwork, collaboration |

> **Motivation** is often described as a fifth component of EI.

---

## 4. Servant Leadership

The leader **shares power** and helps those they lead to perform their best and grow. In agile contexts, assume servant leadership is the appropriate leadership style.

**Four primary servant leader duties:**
1. Keep team members on track; prevent unrelated work from being added
2. Remove impediments (compliance issues, blockers) so the team can move forward
3. Continually communicate the project vision so the team can make good decisions
4. Provide everything the team needs to be productive and motivated (rewards, compensation, support, encouragement)

---

## 5. Centralized vs. Distributed Management

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Centralized** | Team reports to one leader (the PM) |
| **Distributed** | Leadership is shared by several individuals (PM + PM team) or the team is self-organizing |

## 6. Communication Skills

### Flow of Communication
Communication flows:
- **Vertically**: Up and down organizational levels (to/from sponsor, portfolio/program managers, PMO)
- **Horizontally**: Between peers (other PMs, Scrum Masters, team leads)

### Communication Types

| Type | When Used |
|---|---|
| **Formal written** | Charter, planning docs, backlogs, contracts, reports |
| **Formal verbal** | Planned meetings, stakeholder briefings, standups, retrospectives |
| **Informal written** | Email, notes, texts, instant messaging, social media |
| **Informal verbal** | Unscheduled meetings, conversations, casual discussions |

### The Five Cs of Written Communication
1. **Correct** grammar and spelling
2. **Concise** and well-crafted
3. **Clear** and purposeful
4. **Coherent** and logical
5. **Controlled** flow of words and ideas

### Communication Models

**Interactive model** (most comprehensive):
- Sender **encodes** the message → transmits → receiver **decodes** it
- Receiver acknowledges receipt
- **Both** sender and receiver confirm correct interpretation
- **Noise** (cultural differences, distractions, perception) can interfere with decoding

**Sending effectively:**
- Nonverbal cues (gestures, facial expressions, body language) carry a significant portion of in-person communication
- Pitch and tone of voice also convey meaning
- Sender should ask for feedback to confirm understanding: "Can you rephrase what I said?"

**Active listening:**
- Receiver confirms they are listening
- Reflects back what was said
- Expresses agreement or disagreement
- Asks for clarification as needed

### Gulf of Execution vs. Gulf of Evaluation

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Gulf of execution** | Gap between what the user wants and what can actually be implemented |
| **Gulf of evaluation** | Communication gap between the user and the developer — what one hears is different from what they pass on (like a "telephone" game) |

### Communication Blockers
- Noisy surroundings
- Distance between communicators
- Improper encoding of messages
- Language challenges
- Culture

### Communication Technology — Effectiveness Model
(Based on Alistair Cockburn's model)

Two key factors: **interactivity** and **information density**

- **Lowest**: Paper-based documents (low bandwidth, no emotional tone)
- **Highest**: Face-to-face communication at a whiteboard (immediate feedback, nonverbal cues, shortcuts)

> Agile favors face-to-face and high-richness communication.

### Communication Methods

| Method | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Interactive** | Two or more people; reciprocal; feedback expected | Conversations, phone calls, meetings, video calls, instant messaging |
| **Push** | One-way; sender distributes; no feedback expected | Status reports, emails, blogs, memos |
| **Pull** | Information placed centrally; recipients retrieve it | Intranet sites, shared repositories, document libraries |

### Communication Channels Formula

**n(n − 1) / 2** where n = number of stakeholders

| Team size | Channels |
|---|---|
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 10 | 45 |

> Adding one person to a 4-person team adds 4 new channels (from 6 to 10). The increase is non-linear and grows rapidly.

## 7. Motivation Models

### Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Motivation (Daniel Pink)

- **Extrinsic** factors (salary, bonuses) are limited and short-lived motivators
- Once people are fairly compensated, **intrinsic** factors drive motivation

**Three intrinsic motivators:**

| Motivator | Description |
|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Desire to direct one's own work (flexible hours, remote work, choosing projects) |
| **Mastery** | Desire to improve, excel, and do excellent work |
| **Purpose** | Need for meaning — knowing they're making a difference; a clear project vision |

### Theory X, Y, and Z

| Theory | Manager Belief | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **X** | People avoid work, need close supervision, avoid responsibility | Micromanage, control |
| **Y** | People are self-motivated, want to achieve, can self-direct | Empower, trust |
| **Z (Maslow)** | Motivation linked to self-realization, values, and a higher calling | Transcendent |
| **Z (Ouchi)** | Well-being of employees and their families promotes morale/productivity | Job security, family care |

> **PMI-ism:** Assume team members behave according to Theory Y unless told otherwise.

### Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Needs must be satisfied bottom-up — a person cannot move to the next level until the lower levels are met:

| Level | Need |
|---|---|
| 5 (Top) | **Self-actualization** — self-fulfillment, growth, learning |
| 4 | **Esteem** — accomplishment, respect, attention, appreciation |
| 3 | **Social** — love, affection, approval, friends, association |
| 2 | **Safety** — security, stability, freedom from harm |
| 1 (Base) | **Physiological** — air, water, food, housing, clothing |

### McClelland's Theory of Needs (Acquired Needs Theory)

People are most motivated by one of three primary needs:

| Need | Behavior | How to Manage |
|---|---|---|
| **Achievement** | Likes challenging but reachable work; likes recognition | Assign challenging projects; give recognition |
| **Affiliation** | Works best cooperating with others; seeks approval | Put in collaborative team settings |
| **Power** (socially oriented) | Likes to organize and influence others | Allow to manage and lead others |

### Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

| Factor | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Hygiene factors** | Working conditions, salary, security, personal life, relationships, status | Poor hygiene *destroys* motivation; good hygiene does NOT *create* motivation |
| **Motivating agents** | Responsibility, recognition, professional growth, self-actualization | These *truly* motivate people |

> **Key insight:** Fix hygiene problems first (to stop demotivating), then apply motivating agents (to energize).

## 8. Models of Skill Mastery

### Shu-Ha-Ri Model (from martial arts; used in agile)

| Stage | Meaning | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Shu** | "Keep, protect, maintain" | Learning and obeying the rules |
| **Ha** | "Detach or break free" | Mastered through practice; beginning to understand exceptions |
| **Ri** | "Go beyond or transcend" | Rules become second nature; can teach and lead others |

### Dreyfus Model of Adult Skill Acquisition

Five stages from novice to expert:

| Stage | Commitment | Decision-Making | Perspective |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Novice** | Detached | Analytical / follows rules | None |
| **Advanced Beginner** | Detached | Measured planning | Situational |
| **Competent** | Increased | Holistic view | Goals |
| **Proficient** | Committed | Intuitive | Sets priorities |
| **Expert** | Committed | Intuitive | Visionary |

### T-Shaped Skills

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **I-shaped** | Deep specialist in one area only |
| **T-shaped** | Broad range of skills + deep expertise in core area; preferred for agile/hybrid teams |

> T-shaped people are called **generalizing specialists** in agile. They reduce bottlenecks and optimize value.

## 9. Situational Leadership Models

### Situational Leadership II® (Ken Blanchard)

Leadership approach evolves based on two factors:
- **Competence** = knowledge + skills + abilities
- **Commitment** = confidence + motivation

As competence and commitment grow, the leader progresses from:
**Directing → Coaching → Supporting → Delegating**

### OSCAR Model (coaching tool)

| Letter | Stands For | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **O** | Outcome | Individual's long-term goals — what does the team member want? |
| **S** | Situation | Where is the team member now in skill development? |
| **C** | Choices/Consequences | How will they achieve their goals? What are the consequences of each choice? |
| **A** | Actions | The team member's plan of action to achieve goals |
| **R** | Review | Monitor progress; make course corrections |

## 10. Team Development Models

### Tuckman's Ladder of Team Formation

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **Forming** | People come together; lots of questions about purpose, roles |
| **Storming** | Disagreements and conflicts as people learn to work together ("me" thinking) |
| **Norming** | Team builds working relationships and trust; "we" thinking begins |
| **Performing** | Team is efficient; PM can focus on developing individual members |
| **Adjourning** | Project ends; team disbands |

> Teams that have worked together before may skip early stages.

### Drexler/Sibbet Team Performance Model (7 Stages)

| Stage | Theme | Key Artifacts/Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Orientation** | "Why" — project purpose | Kickoff meeting, business case, charter |
| **2. Trust Building** | "Who" — skills and stakeholder info | Team introductions, stakeholder register |
| **3. Goal Clarification** | "What" — requirements, expectations | Requirements, assumptions, acceptance criteria |
| **4. Commitment** | "How" — high-level planning begins | Milestone schedule, release plan, budget, resource needs |
| **5. Implementation** | Detailed planning and execution | Schedule, backlog, scope baseline, release map |
| **6. High Performance** | Team working well together; minimal oversight needed | Established working agreements |
| **7. Renewal** | Changes occur; revisit earlier stages as needed | Retrospectives, replanning |

> The leader's responsibility is to guide stakeholders through each stage and through organizational change.

## 11. Other Leadership Concepts

### Trust
- Trust is built or lost from the first interaction
- A PM must create a **psychologically safe** environment where people can ask questions and share incomplete work without criticism
- Without trust, the team will not follow direction and the project will suffer
- Trust enables cooperation even without formal authority (such as in a matrix org)
- Planning Poker® builds trust through team participation in estimating

### Negotiation
- Provides value in building consensus on project decisions
- Involving team members in decision-making shows the PM values their input

### Influencing
- Begins with actively listening to different viewpoints
- Uses communication and persuasion to develop mutual trust and agreement

### Training
- May be needed for team members to perform or improve
- If training benefits the organization long-term → organizational cost
- If training is project-specific → project cost (documented in resource management plan)

### Coaching
- Helps team members stay on track, improve skills, and achieve goals
- Done at both team level and individual level
- Individual sessions are **confidential**, frank, positive, and followed up on

### Recognition and Rewards
- Determined based on what is **valuable to the individual** being recognized
- Both backward-looking (acknowledge past work) and forward-looking (incentivize ongoing effort)
- The PM should plan recognition and rewards formally; don't leave it to chance

## 12. Conflict Management

### Current View of Conflict

| Old View | New View |
|---|---|
| Conflict is dysfunctional | Conflict is an **inevitable** result of organizational interaction |
| Conflict is to be avoided | Conflict **can be beneficial** — it presents opportunities for improvement |
| Resolved by upper management or separation | Resolved by the **people involved** and their immediate managers |

### Sources of Conflict (in order of frequency)
1. **Schedules** (most common — unrealistic schedules)
2. Project priorities
3. Resources
4. Technical opinions
5. Administrative procedures
6. Cost
7. **Personality** (least common — only becomes personal when root cause isn't resolved)

### Conflict Resolution Guidelines
- Conflict should be resolved by **those involved**, possibly facilitated by the PM
- If PM has no authority over those in conflict, the **sponsor or functional managers** step in
- Exception: if someone is breaking the law or acting unethically → escalate to higher management immediately
- Prevent conflict proactively: clear goals, defined roles, no ambiguity, good planning

### Conflict Resolution Techniques (Thomas-Kilmann Model)

| Technique | Also Called | Description | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collaborating** | Problem-solving | Openly discuss differences; incorporate multiple viewpoints to reach consensus | Win-win (**BEST**) |
| **Compromising** | Reconciling | Both parties give something; partial satisfaction for both | Lose-lose (second best) |
| **Smoothing** | Accommodating | Emphasize agreement; downplay differences; temporary fix | No lasting resolution |
| **Withdrawal** | Avoidance | Retreat or postpone; table the issue | No resolution (use sparingly) |
| **Forcing** | Directing/competing | Push one viewpoint over the other | Win-lose (**usually worst**) |

> **Exam tip:** Look for collaborating/problem-solving as the best answer. Forcing is usually the worst. However, the best answer always depends on the situation described.
